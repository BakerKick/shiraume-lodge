英彦山
Hikosan

A mountain climbed for prayer for thirteen hundred years, and the road our guests take when they walk from the lodge.

It begins at
the bronze gate

Cast in 1637 and given by Nabeshima Katsushige, lord of Saga. Seven metres tall, its pillars more than three metres around, built from bronze cylinders stacked one on the next.

The 「英彦山」 plaque came a century later — granted by Emperor Reigen in 1734.

Then eight hundred
and twenty-four

Five hundred metres of irregular stone cut into the hillside. Cherry low down, cedar above, and terraced walls the whole way up — the ruined lodgings of the mountain ascetics who once lived along this climb.

Stop at the
stone gate

Everyone does. Above it the steps narrow and the roof begins to show through the branches.

And then the
Hōheiden

Rebuilt in 1616 by Hosokawa Tadaoki. Thirty-three metres across, sixteen high, under a shingled roof of sawara cypress that is most of the building.

We keep the bath hot for the way down.
